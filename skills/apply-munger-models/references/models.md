# Model selector

These are working questions, not a universal checklist. Choose models that could change the decision, preferably from different disciplines. The first group reflects themes in Munger's speeches; the rest supplies general analytical tools for multidisciplinary review.

| Lens | Question that changes the decision | Common misuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inversion | How do we reliably fail, and which preventable step starts the chain? | Treating every failure story as likely. |
| Incentives | What does each actor gain from the behavior we expect, including when the stated goal differs? | Assuming everyone is cynical or motivated only by money. |
| Circle of competence | What can we evaluate, and where are we borrowing confidence? | Refusing to learn outside existing expertise. |
| Opportunity cost | What is the best feasible use of the same time, capital and attention? | Comparing with a fantasy alternative. |
| Margin of safety | How much error can the plan tolerate before it fails irreversibly? | Adding costly buffers without measuring the failure mode. |
| Compounding | Which advantage or liability grows as actions repeat, and over what horizon? | Extrapolating growth indefinitely. |
| Scale and economies | What becomes cheaper or more valuable with volume, and what becomes harder? | Calling every product a network effect. |
| Comparative advantage | Who is best placed to do this work relative to their alternatives? | Confusing absolute ability with advantage after tradeoffs. |
| Probabilities and base rates | What happened in comparable cases, and how does this case differ? | Importing an irrelevant reference class. |
| Measurement and accounting | What outcome is counted, and what cost or liability is omitted? | Treating a convenient metric as value created. |
| Regression and variance | How much of an exceptional result reflects noise or selection? | Assuming all results revert or ignoring causal change. |
| Feedback loops | Does the action reinforce or dampen itself through behavior, data, reputation or resources? | Mistaking correlation for a causal loop. |
| Bottlenecks | Which constraint limits the system, and what happens when it is removed? | Optimizing a visible part that is not limiting output. |
| Redundancy and failure modes | Which component can fail, how does failure propagate and what backup has value? | Gold-plating every step. |
| Second-order effects | After the immediate result, how will customers, rivals or teams adapt? | Unbounded speculation. |
| Adaptation and selection | Which actors or strategies survive as conditions change? | Treating biological analogy as empirical proof. |
| Path dependence | Which early choices become expensive to reverse, and which preserve options? | Treating sunk cost as a reason to persist. |
| Systems and thresholds | Is there a nonlinear point where small changes cause a large shift? | Claiming a threshold without evidence. |
| Selection and survivorship | Who is absent from the examples we are studying, and why? | Dismissing useful success cases wholesale. |

## Useful combinations

- **New product wedge:** opportunity cost + incentives + bottleneck + feedback loop + base rate + irreversible dependencies.
- **Organizational change:** incentives + social proof or authority + bottleneck + second-order response + reversal cost.
- **Investment or acquisition:** competence + base rate + opportunity cost + downside or margin of safety + incentive conflicts.
- **Personal commitment:** opportunity cost + compounding + inversion + reversibility + commitment bias.

## Interaction, not model counting

Look for reinforcement, constraint and cancellation. Example: a buyer's incentive slows adoption, preventing the volume required for economies of scale. If the same observation supports several labels, it remains one piece of evidence.
