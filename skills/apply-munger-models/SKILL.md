# Apply Munger models

Help the user make a better decision, not admire a list of models. Treat this as a method inspired by Munger's speeches in *Poor Charlie's Almanack*, especially his cross-disciplinary thinking and *The Psychology of Human Misjudgment*. Do not impersonate Munger, fabricate quotes or present a reconstructed internet list as his official canon.

## Choose the job

- **Decision review:** Compare at least two live options, including doing nothing or waiting when viable. Resolve the actual choice, horizon, constraints and what matters to this user. If a key input is missing, make a labeled assumption or ask one focused question when the answer would reverse the recommendation.
- **Opportunity/strategy review:** Read [opportunities.md](references/opportunities.md). Separate a consequential problem, attractive opportunity, viable wedge and durable business. Make the strongest success and failure cases causal and testable.
- **Observer/review:** Use only accessible decisions, documents or user-provided history. Identify a recurring pattern only with specific examples and a counterexample or limitation. Do not imply background monitoring, scheduled access or a psychological diagnosis. Ask for decision notes if no evidence is available.
- **Decision journal/follow-up:** Read [decision-journal.md](references/decision-journal.md). Record a forecast and observable change-of-mind conditions before the outcome is known. On follow-up, compare with observed outcomes without hindsight rewriting. Do not claim to save or monitor a record unless a file or automation was actually created.
- **Teaching:** Explain a requested model simply with a concrete example and a situation where it misleads. Do not run the full decision procedure for a definitional question.

## Work the decision

1. **Frame the choice.** State the decision in one sentence, the realistic alternatives, objective, deadline, reversibility and downside that cannot be tolerated. Distinguish facts, assumptions and preferences. Check opportunity cost against the best available alternative, not an imaginary ideal.
2. **Build two causal cases before choosing a favorite.** In the forward case, trace how the opportunity produces value, reaches the user or buyer and compounds. Invert separately: describe the most plausible route by which pursuing it wastes scarce resources or causes unacceptable loss. Write each as a short sequence of events, with a key assumption and an observation that would falsify it. Do not mistake a generic pre-mortem for inversion.
3. **Build a small latticework.** Read [models.md](references/models.md) and choose three to six relevant lenses with different causal mechanisms. Start with incentives when people matter; add quantitative, economic, system and operational lenses that could change the choice. For each, say what it predicts and what evidence challenges it. Avoid collecting labels or treating several descriptions of one risk as corroboration.
4. **Check human misjudgment.** Read [misjudgment.md](references/misjudgment.md) and select only plausible tendencies supported by a concrete cue. Check both the user's and analyst's favored conclusions. Treat biases as hypotheses about behavior, never diagnoses.
5. **Analyze interaction.** Name one or two specific chains in which models reinforce, constrain or cancel each other. For each link, mark observed fact versus inference; do not claim a lollapalooza effect merely because several labels appear.
6. **Synthesize and decide.** Compare the best real alternative. Weigh expected upside, downside, evidence quality, reversibility and value of learning. Give a conditional recommendation or identify the missing observation that determines it. A model is a question-generating tool, not proof. Do not manufacture probabilities, base rates or precision.
7. **Make it executable.** Recommend the cheapest test that separates the forward and inverted cases. State who does what, the observable outcome, time boundary and continue, pivot or stop threshold. Prefer customer behavior or actual workflow outcomes over praise, sign-ups or hypothetical intent. For a full review, include the compact record in [decision-journal.md](references/decision-journal.md).

## Output shape

Make a full review comprehensive but layered. Lead with a two- or three-sentence judgment and the biggest uncertainty. Then use short, scannable sections in this order:

1. **The choice:** objective, alternatives, constraints and evidence quality.
2. **The two cases:** success chain and inverted failure chain; include evidence and what would disprove each.
3. **What changes the answer:** the few decisive models and their interactions. Separate observations from inferences.
4. **The call:** recommendation, strongest objection and circumstances that reverse it.
5. **Next move:** one discriminating test with timing, signal and threshold, followed by a brief decision record.

Use concrete numbers when supported; otherwise describe direction and uncertainty. Avoid repetitive model exposition, exhaustive checklists, generic warnings and long prefaces. If the user requests brevity, use two or three decisive lenses and a one-line next test. Do not add a numeric score without a justified scale.

For current markets, prices, regulations, competitors or other changing claims, verify with available sources and cite them. When only the user's account supports a claim, label it accordingly. Distinguish Munger's named tendencies in the primary speech from general analytical models used in the spirit of his latticework. Do not attribute every model to Munger personally.
